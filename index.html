Athena — NSE Quantitative Research System

In Progress · Foundation Phase

A private research system for the Nairobi Securities Exchange, built to bring together clean market data, a signal-testing environment, and a simple language for expressing trading ideas — inspired directly by the discipline learned from WorldQuant BRAIN's alpha research process.

The goal is to make it possible to reliably ask questions of 66 NSE-listed companies' financial and market history, test trading ideas against nearly two decades of data, and eventually let non-programmers write valid signal expressions in plain syntax.

Data Validation Cloud Databases Signal Research System Design
Explain
The Problem

Kenya has markets — 66 listed companies, 13 sectors, nearly two decades of price history, and a growing derivatives market (NEXT) — but it doesn't have the research tools to study them systematically. Every serious NSE researcher today is stuck with manual downloads, spreadsheets, and broker platforms built for placing trades, not generating insight.

Three Root Problems
  • No trusted data foundation — NSE financials are scattered across PDFs, filings, and broker terminals with no cross-validation, so comparing two companies properly takes days before any real analysis starts.
  • No systematic research environment — platforms like WorldQuant BRAIN let you test an idea across hundreds of companies at once; nothing equivalent exists for NSE.
  • Deep market knowledge with no translation layer — Kenyan analysts understand banking dynamics, agricultural cycles, and sector-specific risk deeply, but there's no way to turn that knowledge into a testable, executable signal without also being a software engineer.
Why NEXT Matters

NEXT is Kenya's derivatives exchange — futures on 10 of NSE's largest stocks plus the NSE 25 index. Trading costs are roughly 0.22% per contract versus 0.9%–2.2% for buying shares directly, and it allows both long and short positions — which makes a systematic research edge far more valuable to express here than on the spot market.

Gather
Guiding Principle

No emerging-market dataset is perfect. Rather than waiting for clean data, Athena builds a validation system rigorous enough that imperfect data gets cleaned, flagged, or rejected before it ever reaches a researcher.

Six Data Categories
  • Universe — every listed company, sector, and trading status
  • Prices — daily OHLC and volume, 2007–2025
  • Fundamentals — audited financials for all 66 companies
  • Forensic — the gap between preliminary and final audited results (a signal on management credibility that doesn't exist anywhere else for NSE)
  • Macro — CBK rates, exchange rates, inflation
  • Market Structure — foreign investor flows and NEXT derivatives activity
Validation Standard

Every row of financial data passes automatic accounting checks (assets = liabilities + equity), flags on revenue jumps over 10x year-on-year, and duplicate-filing rejection — running on every ingestion cycle, not just at setup.

Analyze
Value Proposition

A researcher can express an idea in plain language (e.g. "companies whose audited profit is significantly lower than their preliminary reported profit tend to underperform"), test it against 18 years of NSE data, and see exactly how well it worked — before committing any money.

Competitive Moat
  • The audit-delta signal is computed automatically for every company — data nobody else has structured for NSE
  • NEXT has no systematic algorithmic participants yet — a validated signal isn't competing with the market, it's operating in a different dimension from it
  • AEL (Athena Expression Language) lets domain experts write ideas in plain syntax — e.g. "rank companies by return on equity minus non-performing loan ratio" — without needing to code
  • Derivatives cost up to 10x less to trade than direct equity, making signals viable on NEXT that wouldn't be profitable on the spot market
Three Quality Gates

Every signal must pass: (1) the Idea Test — does it have a logical reason to work; (2) the Statistics Test — does the top-ranked group consistently outperform the bottom group across 18 years, not just one good period; (3) the Uniqueness Test — does it add something genuinely new, not just repeat an existing signal.

Deploy
Plan 1 — Foundation (Active Now)

A clean, validated database across all six data categories for all 66 NSE companies. Success: any company's data can be queried and trusted without manual verification.

Plan 2 — Research Engine (Planned)

Signal-testing infrastructure against 18 years of NSE data. Success: the first signal validated with genuine statistical backing.

Plan 3 — Expression Language, AEL (Planned)

Autocomplete, real-time error checking, field documentation in VS Code/Neovim. Success: a non-programmer writes a valid signal on their first session.

Plan 4 — Live Execution (Planned)

Bridges validated signals to live NEXT futures positions. Success: the first systematic trade executed from an Athena-generated signal.

Plan 5 — Platform (Planned)

A subscription platform where researchers own their signals outright — same model as QuantConnect. Success: a second, independently onboarded researcher produces a signal that passes all three quality gates.

VectorDash — CPI Forecast Dashboard

Built July 2025 – March 2026 · Live

Before this existed, checking US inflation data meant manually scanning several different government and financial sites just to see how CPI and its underlying drivers were trending. VectorDash brings all of it — CPI, producer prices, wages, rent, gas prices, and interest rate signals — into one place, with a forecasting model and AI-generated summaries explaining what the numbers actually mean.

It was built to solve a problem I ran into myself: needing a fast, visual way to check inflation trends without digging through raw data every time. I built it end to end through AI-assisted (“vibe”) coding, directing AI from design to deployment to test how far it can take a production tool.

React / TypeScript Forecast Modeling Gemini API FRED API Dashboard Design
Open Live Dashboard
Explain
Problem

There was no single place to see CPI alongside the indicators that actually drive it — producer prices, wages, rent, gas, and interest rate signals. Checking all of it meant manually scanning the Bureau of Labor Statistics, FRED, and financial news, one page at a time.

Communicating It

Instead of just tables, VectorDash uses charts and sparklines to show how the underlying drivers move together, with a click-through deep-dive view for each indicator, and Gemini-generated plain-language summaries explaining what's actually happening.

Gather
Data Sources
  • Headline & Core CPI from FRED
  • Producer Price Index (goods), Average Hourly Earnings (services), Rent/OER (shelter), Gasoline prices (energy)
  • 2-year Treasury yield (monetary stance) and 5/10-year breakeven inflation rates (market expectations)
Handling Messy Data

Economic data doesn't all arrive on the same schedule — PPI comes out before CPI, rent data lags real market rates. The system smooths this out using multi-month trend calculations instead of relying on any single data point.

Analyze
The Forecasting Approach

A top-down model estimates next month's CPI change by combining the trends in wages, producer prices, shelter costs, and gas prices — each weighted by how much it typically moves the overall number. Shelter carries the heaviest weight since it moves CPI the most.

Policy & Expectations

The model also adjusts for interest-rate policy (tight yields pull inflation forecasts down) and long-term market expectations, so forecasts don't just chase recent numbers — they account for where policy is steering things.

Sanity Checking

If the forecast direction disagrees with what the underlying indicators are actually doing, the system flags the inconsistency rather than silently showing a number that doesn't add up.

Deploy
Build

React/TypeScript frontend with an Express backend, integrated directly with the Google Gemini API for narrative summaries.

Live

Deployed and publicly accessible at vectordash.onrender.com, with security basics in place (CORS protection, token verification).

Ongoing

Forecast accuracy gets checked against each new official CPI release, and the model's weightings get recalibrated periodically as the underlying economic data shifts.

WorldQuant BRAIN — Alpha Research

Before Athena, I spent time in WorldQuant BRAIN's research environment, where the challenge is to design trading signals ("alphas") and prove — with strict testing rules — that they actually hold up on real market data. I haven't reached Consultant status yet, but the discipline from this process shaped how Athena is being built today. Here are the two alphas I submitted and passed:

Earnings Expectations Gap

This signal compares how a company's earnings actually came in against what was expected, then tracks whether that gap has been trending wider or narrower over roughly the last two years. The idea: companies that keep beating expectations by a growing margin tend to keep being underestimated by the market, and vice versa.

Expression (BRAIN fastexpr) 
// Rank every company by the gap below, from lowest to highest
alpha = rank(
  winsorize(
    // How a company's ACTUAL earnings effect has ranked
    // against its own history over the last ~2 years (504 trading days)
    ts_rank(ts_backfill(vec_avg(ern4_erneffct1), 3), 504)

    // minus how the FORECASTED earnings effect has ranked
    // against its own history over the same period
    - ts_rank(ts_backfill(vec_avg(ern4_fcsterneffct), 3), 504),

    // Cap extreme outlier values so a few wild numbers
    // don't distort the whole ranking
    std=3
  )
);

In plain terms: this measures the gap between where a company's real earnings performance stands versus where analyst forecasts say it should stand — then ranks every company by the size of that gap.

Accrual Decomposition

This one looks for early warning signs in how a company manages its books — specifically, unusual buildups in inventory paired with unusually fast collection of money owed to it. That combination can be a sign of earnings quality problems, so the signal shorts companies showing this pattern and favors those that don't.

Expression (BRAIN fastexpr) 
-group_neutralize(
    // Year-over-year change in inventory, scaled by total assets —
    // a big unexplained inventory buildup can be a red flag
    winsorize(
        ts_delta(inventory, 252) / assets,
        std=2
    ) *
    // Year-over-year change in money owed to the company, scaled
    // by assets, and flipped in sign — receivables shrinking faster
    // than expected can also flag aggressive accounting
    winsorize(
        -ts_delta(receivable, 252) / assets,
        std=2
    ) *
    // Only apply this to companies that are actually profitable
    (operating_income > 0),

    // Compare each company only against others in its own
    // sub-industry, so the ranking isn't skewed by sector-wide trends
    subindustry
);

In plain terms: this is a classic "quality of earnings" check. Companies piling up inventory and moving through receivables in an unusual way tend to be quietly managing their numbers — so the signal bets against those and favors companies with cleaner accounting patterns, comparing fairly within the same industry group.
